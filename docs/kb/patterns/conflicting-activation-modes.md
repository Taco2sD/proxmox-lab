# Two mechanisms start the same thing

## What happens

A daemon can be started more than one way: by its own main process (a monitor that spawns its
helpers), by systemd socket activation, by a timer, by another service. When the configuration
enables two of them, one loses: it fails at every boot, or both run and fight over a socket or file.
The service often works anyway (the winner does the job), so nobody notices until something counts
failed units.

## How to spot it

- A `.socket` unit failed (exit status 17, "Address already in use") while the service works.
- `systemctl list-dependencies --reverse <unit>` shows who pulls it in; `systemctl cat` both units.
- The same failed unit on every host of a kind at once means configuration, not a fault:
  `count by (name) (node_systemd_unit_state{state="failed"} == 1) > 1`

## How to fix

Pick one mechanism (the one upstream recommends for the version in use) and disable or mask the
other explicitly in the role that configures the daemon, with a comment saying why. When the
service is critical (logins), prefer the smallest change that keeps today's behaviour.

## How to prevent

When a role configures a daemon, check `systemctl list-unit-files '<daemon>*'` on a fresh clone:
every unit that ships enabled is either used or disabled on purpose.
