# Per-connection units pile up as failed

## What happens

A socket with `Accept=yes` starts one instance of a template service (`name@<n>-<addresses>.service`)
per connection. Every instance that exits non-zero stays loaded in the `failed` state until someone
runs `systemctl reset-failed`. A client that retries (see [client-retry-loop](client-retry-loop.md))
turns that into hundreds or thousands of failed units: `systemctl --failed` becomes useless, and
alerts on failed units fire for a condition nobody can act on unit by unit.

## How to spot it

- `systemctl list-units --failed --no-legend | wc -l` far above a handful, mostly one template.
- PromQL: `count by (host) (node_systemd_unit_state{state="failed",name=~".+@.+"} == 1)`
- `journalctl -u 'name@*' --since -1h | grep -c 'Failed with result'` gives the rate.

## How to fix

Add `CollectMode=inactive-or-failed` to the template's `[Unit]` section: systemd unloads each
instance when it ends, failed or not, and its log stays in the journal. Clear the backlog once with
`systemctl reset-failed 'name@*'`. Then find out why the instances fail (usually the client).

## How to prevent

Every `Accept=yes` socket in this repo gets `CollectMode=inactive-or-failed` on its template, and
the service exits 0 for expected conditions (the target is deliberately off) with a clear log line.
The failed-units alert counts per host, so a pile-up shows as one large number, not an alert storm.
