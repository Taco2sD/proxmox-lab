# Two automated jobs need the same lock, and one gives up

## What happens

Two pieces of automation that are each fine on their own share an exclusive lock: apt's
(`/var/lib/dpkg/lock-frontend`, `/var/lib/apt/lists/lock`), a Terraform/OpenTofu state
lock, a flock around a script. When their schedules overlap, the second one waits a
short, hard-coded time and then fails. Neither job is broken, so testing either alone
never shows it; it shows up as an occasional failed run at the same time of day, or on a
machine's first boot, when every catch-up job runs at once.

## How to spot it

- Failures that cluster around another job's schedule (apt: 06:00-07:00 for
  `apt-daily-upgrade.timer`, and the first hour after a clone's first boot).
- Messages such as `Failed to lock apt for exclusive operation`, `Could not get lock
  /var/lib/dpkg/lock-frontend`, `Error acquiring the state lock`.
- On a workstation: `journalctl -u ansible-pull -u lab-firstboot | grep -i lock`; on
  mgmt01: `grep -il "lock" /var/log/lab/*.log`.
- Who holds it right now: `fuser -v /var/lib/dpkg/lock-frontend /var/lib/apt/lists/lock`.

## How to fix

Make the waiting side wait long enough for the other job's longest normal run, in one
place that covers every caller (for apt in Ansible: a play-level `module_defaults` with
`lock_timeout`, not a value on each task). Where one job matters more (enrolling a new
machine), sequence the other behind it rather than racing it.

## How to prevent

When adding a scheduled job, list what it locks and what else takes that lock, and say in
a comment who waits for whom. Test the waiting side with the lock held on purpose
(KB-0150 shows how).
